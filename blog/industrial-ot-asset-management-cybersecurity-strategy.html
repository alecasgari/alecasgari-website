Securing Industrial Operations Through Better Visibility

Achieving industrial cybersecurity requires moving beyond firewalls to total visibility. Leaders must prioritize a proactive asset management strategy to be secure.

Alec Asgari
7/18/2026

The Foundation of Operational Resilience

Industrial cybersecurity is no longer a peripheral IT concern; it is a fundamental pillar of organizational viability. Leaders often focus on digital security, yet fail to account for the physical-digital convergence in their factories and critical infrastructure. The central problem is a visibility gap: you cannot protect an asset you have not identified, documented, and integrated into your security framework. Achieving robust operational technology (OT) security starts with a comprehensive understanding of every connected device, from legacy controllers to modern sensors, rather than relying on firewalls alone.

The Mechanism of Exposure

The risks to industrial infrastructure often arise from a mismatch between enterprise IT security standards and the reality of the plant floor. Industrial environments frequently rely on equipment that was never designed for modern network connectivity. When these assets are connected to wider corporate networks for monitoring or automation, they introduce entry points that legacy security tools often ignore.

This problem is compounded when organizational siloes separate the IT teams, who manage the digital network, from the OT teams, who manage the physical machinery. Without a unified asset management strategy, there is no shared accountability for identifying vulnerabilities or updating firmware. This creates a blind spot where unauthorized or unpatched devices can persist on the network, effectively creating an open door for security threats.

Illustrative Scenario: Bridging the Asset Gap

Consider an illustrative scenario at a manufacturing plant experiencing frequent unplanned downtime. Initially, the plant manager and the IT department operate in separate silos. The IT team manages server updates, while the engineering team manages the PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers) on the floor.

When a system anomaly occurs, the teams struggle to identify the cause because the engineering team lacks visibility into network traffic, and the IT team lacks knowledge of the physical equipment. Under a redesigned approach, the organization establishes an integrated asset inventory. Both teams now access a shared dashboard that maps every sensor and controller.

When a newly installed sensor begins communicating with an external IP address, the system flags the activity immediately. The engineering lead reviews the alert to confirm the sensor's purpose, while the IT security lead investigates the network configuration. The human-AI workflow here is clear: the AI handles the continuous monitoring and traffic analysis, while the human teams retain full decision-making power to determine if the activity is a operational requirement or a security threat. Accountability remains fixed with the managers, who must authorize any changes to the network access policy.

Practical Framework for OT Asset Management

Building resilience requires a deliberate transition from reactive patching to proactive management. Leaders can follow this framework to gain and maintain operational visibility:

  • Map the landscape: Perform a comprehensive inventory of all connected industrial assets. Identify hardware versions, firmware levels, and network connectivity status for every device.
  • Classify by criticality: Evaluate each asset based on its role in the primary business process. Distinguish between assets that support non-essential monitoring and those that are vital to production continuity.
  • Establish integrated governance: Create a cross-functional team that includes both IT security and OT engineering. Ensure both groups share responsibility for the security posture of the industrial network.
  • Implement continuous monitoring: Move away from manual, point-in-time audits. Utilize automated systems that alert the team to new devices as they connect to the network.
  • Refine response workflows: Define clear procedures for how the team will handle security alerts. Distinguish between automated shutdowns for high-risk threats and manual review processes for operational anomalies.

Diagnostic Checklist for Leaders

Use this checklist to assess the maturity of your industrial security and asset management processes:

  • Do you have a real-time, accurate list of every device connected to your industrial network?
  • Can your team distinguish between routine operational traffic and suspicious network activity?
  • Is there a clear, documented process for updating firmware on legacy industrial equipment?
  • Are IT and OT personnel empowered to collaborate on security decisions, or do they operate in isolation?
  • Is the security of your production floor a standard agenda item in executive risk reviews?

Risks, Trade-offs, and Human Judgment

Adopting a visibility-first approach to security is not without challenges. An automated asset management system, while useful, is not a substitute for rigorous human judgment. Over-reliance on automation can lead to automation bias, where teams trust system alerts implicitly rather than investigating the root cause.

Furthermore, intensive monitoring can sometimes interfere with sensitive industrial hardware if not properly configured. Organizations must balance the need for visibility with the operational requirements of the equipment. Governance remains critical; technology provides the data, but human leaders must interpret that data to make decisions that prioritize both security and operational performance. Finally, the workforce transition is significant. Employees accustomed to isolated systems may resist new monitoring tools. Success depends on clear communication regarding why visibility is necessary for the long-term safety and stability of their work environment.

Conclusion

Industrial cybersecurity is a foundational element of a modern, efficient organization. The shift toward total visibility of operational technology assets is the necessary first step in protecting critical infrastructure from emerging risks. Leaders must move beyond siloed management and foster a collaborative environment where security and operations share responsibility for the network. Start by prioritizing a full inventory of your connected assets and integrating the teams responsible for their oversight. Proactive, visible management is the only reliable way to maintain resilience in an increasingly connected industrial landscape.
